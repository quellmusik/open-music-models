# Open-Source / Locally-Runnable AI Music Generation Models — Comparison as of late October 2026

*Research date: 31 Oct 2026 (pages crawled 31 Oct 2026 22:11–22:13 UTC unless dated otherwise). Flags: **[verified live]** = primary repo/model page fetched this run; **[index]** = search-index / secondary source only. Vendor benchmark claims are labelled as such throughout — do not quote them as independent findings.*

## Summary

- **There is still no open Suno.** The only genuinely independent head-to-head found that ran the models itself (IT-JIM, May 2026, 8 models × 3 prompt/lyric sets, 1 seed each) concludes: *"no, there is not an open-source Suno yet"* — the closest gets *"within audible-but-real distance of Suno V5 on some prompts."* Every "beats Suno v5/v6" headline in 2026 (YuE2, ACE-Step 1.5, LeVo 2, HeartMuLa-7B) is **self-reported, usually on a benchmark built by the same team**.
- **Quality winner (independent, May 2026 slate, pre-YuE2): LeVo 2 / SongGeneration 2** — most natural on hard-rock, jazz and pop — **but its Tencent custom licence forbids all commercial/production use.**
- **Best all-round shippable vocal model: ACE-Step 1.5 (+ XL)** — MIT code *and* weights, explicit commercial grant, fastest, lowest VRAM, only model with official Mac/AMD/Intel/CPU paths and a full edit/cover/repaint toolkit. Independent caveat: a persistent "metallic shimmer" on vocals and cymbals.
- **Newest frontier claimant: YuE2-3B (9 Sep 2026)** — editable ABC score → 48 kHz stereo song, zero-shot covers, agentic editing. Its WildSongBench win over Suno v5/v6 is **author-built and author-run** (and YuE2's own README concedes the gap *"does not establish statistical significance"*). Weights are **CC BY-NC 4.0 + a creator permission**: individuals/musicians may monetise outputs free; **companies need a paid licence** — a regression from YuE v1's Apache 2.0.
- **Instrumental landscape changed in May 2026:** **Stable Audio 3** (Small/Small-SFX/Medium open weights) supersedes Stable Audio Open 1.0/1.5 — up to 6 min 20 s, inpainting, and a Small model that is **officially CPU-capable** — making it the only serious answer for the no-GPU machines in §11–12.
- **Machine (A) (i5 / 16 GB / Intel iGPU only):** realistically **ACE-Step 1.5 (CPU/XPU, turbo, no LM, short-to-medium songs, slow) for vocals** and **Stable Audio 3 Small-Music / MusicGen-Small for instrumentals**. Everything else vocal is CUDA-first and either won't run or will be unusably slow / experimentally broken on Intel Vulkan.
- **Machine (B) (2 vCPU / 4 GB VPS):** **no full-song vocal model runs.** At best, tiny instrumental experiments (Stable Audio Open Small / Stable Audio 3 Small quantised) for ~10–30 s clips, with swap, at minutes-per-clip — not a production route.
- **Rented RTX 4090:** RunPod Community **$0.34/hr**, Secure **$0.89/hr**; Vast.ai median **~$0.49/hr**, floor ~$0.41 (Oct 2026). Setup/download dominates: **first song all-in ≈ $0.09–0.14 (Community) / $0.13–0.20 (Vast median) / $0.23–0.36 (Secure)**; marginal songs thereafter are **< $0.01–0.03 each**.

### 3–5. Per-model short notes (supplementing the tables)

- **YuE v1** — Two-stage LLaMA-style: 7B stage-1 (vocals+accompaniment tokens, CoT or in-context-learning variants per language) + 1B stage-2 + upsampler to 44.1 kHz. Its lasting contribution (per IT-JIM) is segment-based conditioning (~14 sections/song), adopted by every later full-song model. Practical killer: speed. Community forks exist *because* of it: **YuEGP** (deepbeepmeep, mmgp offloading; profiles 16 GB → 12 GB 8-bit → <10 GB sequential → minimal-VRAM profile 5; a documented RTX 3060 12 GB run took **~90 min for a >2-min, 6-section song**, vs ~4 min on a 4090 at profile 1 **[index]**) and **YuE-exllamav2** (claims 500%+ speedup, CUDA 12.4 / ROCm 6.2.4 **[index]**). A third-party GGUF of stage-1 alone also circulates — **it cannot generate songs by itself** (no tokeniser/stage-2 in llama.cpp) **[index]**.
- **YuE2** — Architecturally a *new* model in the old repo: one AR–NAR Mixture-of-Transformers predicts an ABC score + semantic tokens, flow-matching produces acoustic latents, VAE decodes. Also ships MERT2 (music understanding) and SheetSage2 (audio→score, for covers). FLAC output with score/tokens/latents retained per song. Official route is deliberately narrow (Linux + 24 GB NVIDIA); everything broader (Windows/Vulkan/CPU/GGUF) is the community **YuE2 Studio / yue2.cpp** path (§9).
- **ACE-Step v1 vs 1.5** — v1 (3.5B, Apache 2.0, diffusion + Sana DCAE) is *cleaner-sounding* per IT-JIM but musically weaker; 1.5 (hybrid LM-planner + DiT, FSQ 5 Hz tokeniser) is far more capable/tooled but carries the metallic artifact IT-JIM attributes (hypothesis, unablated) to the new codec/tokeniser or low step counts. If pristine audio matters and editing doesn't, test v1 alongside 1.5.
- **HeartMuLa** — A *family*: HeartMuLa LM (Llama-based, 12.5 Hz HeartCodec — lowest frame rate in the slate), HeartTranscriptor (lyrics ASR), HeartCLAP. Paper (arXiv:2601.10547) evaluation is self-run and its PER uses its *own* transcriptor as judge (IT-JIM caveat). IT-JIM listening: *"generic pop in every case"* across rock/jazz/pop — framed by them as possibly a checkpoint/inference quirk, but consistent across prompts. YuE2's (author-run) WildSongBench puts HeartMuLa at 6.2483, mid-pack, ahead of ACE-Step 1.5 (6.0118) and DiffRhythm 2 (5.2428).
- **LeVo 2** — Hybrid LeLM (~4B) + diffusion codec, with the slate's most aggressive preference pipeline (three offline DPO axes merged by weight interpolation in v1; ~200k pairs + semi-online aesthetic DPO in v2). That pipeline plausibly *is* why it sounds best independently — and the licence is why most readers cannot ship it. **Any commercial shortlist must exclude LeVo/LeVo 2 despite the quality win.**
- **DiffRhythm line** — v1: first open diffusion full-song model, still the speed king per-minute. v1.2 (May 2025) is the one to cite for editing/continuation. **DiffRhythm+** (DPO, expanded data) sits between them in the v1 repo lineage. v2 trades some speed for lyric alignment (block flow matching); its own paper (§5.2/§6, per IT-JIM) admits vocals trail ACE-Step/LeVo and *"open-source still falls short of commercial systems overall"* — the most honest paper in the set.
- **SongBloom / SongGen / InspireMusic** — All three are 2025 research releases whose *released artefacts* are narrower than their papers: SongBloom needs a reference clip and caps at 150–240 s; SongGen caps at 30 s/English/16 kHz; InspireMusic shipped only instrumental checkpoints (its "song" model never got a download link). None has a 2026 release. Include for completeness, recommend none as a starting point in Oct 2026.

### 6. Quality standing — vendor claims vs independent evidence

**Vendor / self-reported (do not present as independent):**
- YuE2 best-of-8 **6.9632 SongBench Avg on WildSongBench (192 prompts, 12 Sep 2026)** > Mureka 9 (6.9377) > Suno v5 (6.8721) > Suno v5.5 (6.7150) > Suno v6 (6.5562); standard YuE2 6.7316; PER 8.44% (standard) / 9.79% (best-of-8 — note best-of-8 selects on a SongBench dimension being reported). Benchmark, bench team and model team are the same lab; README itself notes the top gap is not statistically established. **[verified live]**
- ACE-Step 1.5: *"quality beyond most commercial models"*, SongEval **8.09 vs Suno v5 7.87** in launch coverage; its own tutorial phrasing is more modest — *"between Suno v4.5 and v5."* Note the **cross-benchmark contradiction**: on YuE2's WildSongBench, ACE-Step 1.5 scores near the bottom of the 2026 open set (6.0118). Both numbers are team-run on team-favoured benches. **[verified live + index]**
- LeVo 2: PER **8.55%** vs Suno v5 12.4% / Mureka v8 9.96%, and a 20-professional / 100-song expert panel — all README-only, test set and ASR undisclosed (IT-JIM explicitly declines to endorse the PER comparison).
- HeartMuLa: *"internal 7B achieves comparable performance with Suno"* — **the 7B is unreleased**, so this is unverifiable by construction. The released claim is oss-3B "best open model for lyrics controllability" (Feb 2026, self-assessed).
- DiffRhythm 2: *"outperforms existing open-source models in subjective and objective evaluations"* (paper, self-run; 10 pro listeners per IT-JIM's count of the slate's typical eval sizes).

**Independent evidence (the only head-to-head that ran the models):**
- **IT-JIM (21 May 2026)** — slate: ACE v1, ACE 1.5, LeVo 2, Khala, YuE, HeartMuLa, Muse, DiffRhythm 2. Method limits: 3 prompts, 1 seed/model, cheap cloud GPU, listening pass (not a powered benchmark). Findings: **LeVo 2 most natural overall; ACE 1.5 best substrate/tooling with a structural metallic artifact; v1 cleaner than 1.5 but weaker musically; HeartMuLa style-flat; YuE unremarkable + very slow; DiffRhythm 2 most style-responsive, rhythm-unstable; no model = Suno.** YuE2, MuLaCover and Stable Audio 3 all postdate this test — **there is no independent YuE2 evaluation in the sources found**, only community listening reports described as "mixed" in secondary reviews **[index]**.
- General benchmark caveat (IT-JIM, worth quoting in any report): WER/PER are computed by an ASR that may itself favour a model; SongEval is published by the DiffRhythm team, MuQ-MuLan by Tencent's lab, WildSongBench/SongBench by the YuE lab, HeartMuLa's PER by its own transcriptor. *"None of these patterns mean the numbers are wrong. They mean reading them should include who built the metric."*

**Community reputation (Oct 2026, secondary):** YuE2 was the September breakout (HF global trending #3 on 17 Sep, #1 text-to-audio on 20 Sep **[verified live]**; heavy ComfyUI/Studio adoption). ACE-Step 1.5 is the default recommendation for local/self-hosted use in Oct 2026 comparison roundups. HeartMuLa retains a "lyrical/expressive, best lyrics control" niche reputation in lighter-weight roundups that conflicts with IT-JIM's flat-style finding — report both.

### 7. Licences — the traps

| Trap | Detail |
|---|---|
| **YuE v1 ≠ YuE2** | v1: Apache 2.0 code+weights (commercial OK). v2: Apache code, **CC BY-NC weights + creator permission** — *individual* creators/musicians may monetise outputs free; *companies* must license the weights. Old YuE v1 HF cards / mirrors still showing CC BY-NC are stale relative to the 30 Jan 2025 Apache switch **[index conflict — see §15]** |
| **LeVo 1 & 2** | Tencent custom licence in the repo LICENSE file governs, **not** the HF card (`unknown`). Academic/research/education only; commercial *or production* use forbidden "under any circumstances" |
| **MusicGen / AudioCraft** | Code MIT, **weights CC BY-NC 4.0** — the most commonly mis-cited licence in the field |
| **ACE-Step 1.5** | Cleanest: MIT code+weights, explicit commercial-output grant. (Community fine-tunes differ — e.g. ACE-Step Studio notes a "Regrind" fine-tune under CC BY-NC-SA) |
| **Stability family** | Community Licence = free commercial use **only below $1M annual revenue**; ≥$1M needs Enterprise. SA Open 1.0's *launch* terms were stricter (non-commercial research) — version-pin what you download. SA3 Medium additionally gated by **Gemma terms** for its text encoder |
| **DiffRhythm VAE** | Repo licence is Apache 2.0 for "code and DiT weights"; the VAE derives from Stable Audio Open — redistribution/commercial analysis should check that component separately |
| **Muse** | Licences are permissive, but the *training data* is Suno-v5 output; derivative-use legality under Suno's ToS is unaddressed |
| **TangoFlux / Mustango** | Neither is a clean permissive licence on the evidence found (Stability-community/data restrictions for TangoFlux; Mustango licence unverified) — do not assume Apache |

### 12. Fit B — Linux VPS, 2 vCPU, 4 GB RAM, no GPU

**Verdict: nothing in the full-song/vocal class runs. Do not plan around this machine for generation.**

- **Hard blockers:** PyTorch + runtime alone is ~2–3 GB RSS/disk-heavy; every vocal model is ≥0.6B parameters *plus* a codec/VAE *plus* a text encoder, and all assume CUDA. Even the smallest vocal model found, **Muse (0.6B)**, has no CPU/VPS deployment documentation and its full pipeline (MuCodec etc.) will not fit a 4 GB envelope with any headroom.
- **Borderline experiments only (instrumental, short, slow):** **Stable Audio Open Small** (341M, designed for phones, ~11 s clips) or **Stable Audio 3 Small** in a quantised GGUF/ONNX derivative (~640 MB INT4 weights in community repackagings **[index]**) *might* complete a 10–30 s clip on 2 vCPU with swap enabled, at minutes-per-clip. The upstream SA3 Small F32/BF16 stack (459M DiT + 108M SAME-S + T5Gemma encoder) should be assumed to **exceed or thrash** 4 GB. MusicGen-Small CPU is likewise a swap-thrash risk at 4 GB (300M model, but EnCodec + T5 + PyTorch overhead).
- **Practical recommendation to state in the report:** use the VPS as a *controller* (queueing, storage, serving files) and generate via (a) free hosted demos/Spaces, (b) a per-minute/per-song API, or (c) the rented GPU in §13 spun up on demand. A 4 GB VPS is also too small to be the download/staging host for most checkpoints comfortably once OS + cache are counted (YuE2 set alone is 4–10 GB on disk; ACE XL sets reach ~18.5 GB in studio packaging **[verified live]**).
