There is still no open Suno.

Every open and locally-runnable music model worth knowing — 14 full-song vocal models and 9 instrumental ones — compared on quality, licence, hardware, and speed, then fitted against three real machines: your Dell laptop, your server, and a rented RTX 4090. The only genuinely independent head-to-head that actually ran the models (IT-JIM, May 2026) concludes the closest open model gets “within audible-but-real distance of Suno V5 on some prompts.” Every 2026 headline claiming an open model “beats Suno v5/v6” — YuE2, ACE-Step 1.5, LeVo 2, HeartMuLa-7B — is self-reported, usually on a benchmark built by the same team.

Best you can actually ship: ACE-Step 1.5

MIT code and weights with an explicit commercial grant, the fastest generation in class, the lowest VRAM, the richest editing toolkit (cover, repaint, stem extract, multi-track) — and the only vocal model with official Intel/CPU paths. Independent caveat: a persistent “metallic shimmer” on vocals and cymbals.

Best-sounding: LeVo 2 — which you can’t use commercially

The independent quality winner (most natural on hard-rock, jazz and pop) ships under a Tencent licence that forbids all commercial and production use. YuE2-3B is the frontier claimant behind it, with an editable musical score — and a licence that lets individual musicians monetise outputs free, but makes companies pay.

Your hardware, honestly

The Dell runs ACE-Step 1.5 (slowly, CPU) and Stable Audio 3 Small for instrumentals. The 4 GB server runs no vocal model at all. A rented 4090 changes everything: first song all-in ≈ $0.09–0.36, extra songs ≈ $0.003–0.023 each.

Fit: your three machines

The same models, judged against the hardware you actually have — plus the rented-GPU route that bypasses the hardware question entirely.

The Dell laptop

WINDOWS · INTEL i5 · 16 GB RAM · INTEGRATED INTEL GRAPHICS ONLY

One vocal model is realistic, plus two instrumental ones. Everything else is no.

  • Yes — best vocal optionACE-Step 1.5 Officially supports Intel XPU and CPU; the ≤6 GB tier is “turbo + no LM + INT8 + full CPU offload”, and a GGML port (acestep.cpp, GGUF turbo Q8_0 ≈2.4 GB) runs on CPU/Vulkan. Run the 2B turbo model at 8 steps, no LM planner (or the 0.6B LM at most), short durations first (30–120 s), batch 1. No source benchmarks this exact hardware — extrapolating from its 3090 and M2-Max-class figures, expect minutes to tens of minutes per song. That is an estimate, not a measurement. 16 GB RAM is the binding constraint; integrated graphics shares system RAM, so treat this as a CPU job. Quality note: turbo without the LM is ACE-Step’s weakest configuration, on top of the known metallic artifact.
  • Yes — best instrumentalStable Audio 3 Small-Music Officially CPU-capable by design (459M + 108M). The paper summary has 120 s rendered in 5.92 s on a Mac CPU — a Windows i5 will be slower (different CPU, no CoreML), but this model class is phone/laptop-scale and fits comfortably in 16 GB. The only model in this report where the Dell is a supported configuration rather than a fallback.
  • Yes — clips onlyMusicGen Small (300M) CPU inference via Transformers/AudioCraft is a long-established path; ~30 s max per call at 32 kHz, minutes-per-clip class on CPU (no exact i5 figure sourced). Medium/Large on CPU are not realistic. Non-commercial weights.
  • MarginalAudioLDM2 · TangoFlux · Mustango Diffusers CPU-offload exists for AudioLDM2; TangoFlux’s ComfyUI nodes expose CPU offload. 10–30 s clips only, slow (tens of minutes worst case, unverified on this hardware) — and their licences are non-commercial/restrictive anyway. Low value for the effort.
  • Don’tYuE2 via YuE2 Studio It technically launches, but: “Without a GPU the engine falls back to the processor, which works but is many times slower”, and Intel/AMD Vulkan is experimental — on AMD integrated graphics the song came out with unintelligible vocals, and discrete Intel is untested. Integrated Intel is the worst-tested category. If attempted anyway: Q5_K_M set (4.0 GB), CPU not Vulkan, short songs, very long renders (the 4090 does 3:38 in 46 s; CPU fallback is described only as “many times slower”).
  • NoYuE v1 / YuEGP · HeartMuLa · LeVo 1/2 · DiffRhythm 1/2 · SongGen · SongBloom · InspireMusic All CUDA/FlashAttention-first with no Intel-iGPU or CPU path documented (YuEGP is CUDA-only by design). Sizes of 3–4B plus codec, against 16 GB RAM, rule out heroic CPU attempts; SongBloom’s documented Mac-MPS path does not transfer to Intel iGPU on Windows. Use their hosted demos, or the rented GPU below.

The server (VPS)

LINUX · 2 vCPU · 4 GB RAM · NO GPU

Nothing in the full-song/vocal class runs. Do not plan around this machine for generation.

  • NoEvery vocal model PyTorch + runtime alone is ~2–3 GB; every vocal model is ≥0.6B parameters plus a codec/VAE plus a text encoder, and all assume CUDA. Even the smallest vocal model found, Muse (0.6B), has no CPU/VPS deployment documentation and its full pipeline will not fit a 4 GB envelope with any headroom.
  • Borderline experiments onlyStable Audio Open Small · Stable Audio 3 Small (quantised) The 341M phone-class SA Open Small, or SA3 Small in a quantised GGUF/ONNX derivative (~640 MB INT4 weights in community repackagings), might complete a 10–30 s instrumental clip on 2 vCPU with swap enabled, at minutes-per-clip. The upstream SA3 Small F32/BF16 stack should be assumed to exceed or thrash 4 GB; MusicGen-Small on CPU is likewise a swap-thrash risk.
  • Right roleController, not generator Use the VPS to queue jobs, store and serve files — and generate via free hosted demos, a per-song API, or the rented GPU spun up on demand. It’s also too small to comfortably stage most checkpoints: the YuE2 set alone is 4–10 GB on disk, and ACE-Step XL sets reach ~18.5 GB in studio packaging.

05

Quality: claims vs. evidence

Almost every impressive number in this field was produced by the team selling the model, on a benchmark that team built. The split below is the whole story — read the vendor column as advertising and the independent column as evidence.

Vendor / self-reported claims — treat as advertising

  • YuE2 — best-of-8 score of 6.9632 on WildSongBench, ahead of Mureka 9 (6.9377), Suno v5 (6.8721), Suno v5.5 (6.7150) and Suno v6 (6.5562). The benchmark, the bench team and the model team are the same lab — and YuE2’s own README concedes the top gap “does not establish statistical significance.” Note also that best-of-8 selects on a dimension being reported.
  • ACE-Step 1.5 — “quality beyond most commercial models”; SongEval 8.09 vs Suno v5’s 7.87 in launch coverage. Its own tutorial phrasing is more modest: “between Suno v4.5 and v5.” On YuE2’s bench, ACE-Step 1.5 scores near the bottom of the 2026 open set (6.0118) — the cross-benchmark contradiction in one line.
  • LeVo 2 — lyric-error rate (PER) 8.55% vs Suno v5’s 12.4% and Mureka v8’s 9.96%, plus a 20-professional / 100-song expert panel. All README-only; test set and ASR undisclosed.
  • HeartMuLa — “internal 7B achieves comparable performance with Suno.” The 7B is unreleased, so this is unverifiable by construction. Its released self-assessment is oss-3B as “best open model for lyrics controllability” (Feb 2026) — scored using its own transcriptor as the judge.
  • DiffRhythm 2 — “outperforms existing open-source models in subjective and objective evaluations” (its paper, self-run).

Independent evidence — the one test that ran the models

  • IT-JIM (21 May 2026) ran 8 open models × 3 prompt/lyric sets, 1 seed each, on a cheap cloud GPU, and listened. Method limits are real — it’s a listening pass, not a powered benchmark — but it is the only head-to-head in the sources that isn’t run by a model’s own team.
  • LeVo 2 most natural overall across hard-rock, jazz and pop.
  • ACE-Step 1.5 best substrate and tooling, with a structural metallic artifact on vocals/cymbals; v1 sounds cleaner but is musically weaker.
  • HeartMuLa — “generic pop in every case” across rock/jazz/pop, framed by IT-JIM as possibly a checkpoint/inference quirk but consistent across prompts. (Lighter-weight roundups keep a “lyrical/expressive, best lyrics control” reputation for it — both views are reported here because they conflict.)
  • YuE v1 unremarkable and very slow; DiffRhythm 2 most style-responsive but rhythm-unstable.
  • YuE2, MuLaCover and Stable Audio 3 all postdate this test. There is no independent YuE2 evaluation in the sources found — only community listening reports described as “mixed” in secondary reviews.
“None of these patterns mean the numbers are wrong. They mean reading them should include who built the metric.”

— IT-JIM, on the field’s benchmarks: WER/PER are computed by an ASR that may itself favour a model; SongEval is published by the DiffRhythm team, MuQ-MuLan by Tencent’s lab, WildSongBench/SongBench by the YuE lab, and HeartMuLa’s PER by its own transcriptor.

06

Licence traps

The field’s licences are where good projects go to become unusable. These are the specific mismatches the research found between what people assume and what the licence files actually say.

YuE v1 ≠ YuE2

v1 is Apache 2.0 for code and weights — commercial use is fine. v2 keeps Apache code but moves the weights to CC BY-NC 4.0 plus a creator permission: individual creators and musicians may monetise outputs free; companies must license the weights. Old YuE v1 model cards and mirrors still showing CC BY-NC are stale relative to the 30 Jan 2025 Apache switch.

LeVo 1 & 2: the quality winner you can’t ship

The Tencent custom licence in the repo’s LICENSE file governs — not the Hugging Face card, which says license: unknown. Academic, research and education use only; commercial or production use is forbidden “under any circumstances.” Any commercial shortlist must exclude LeVo despite the quality win.

MusicGen: MIT code, non-commercial weights

AudioCraft’s code is MIT, but the MusicGen weights are CC BY-NC 4.0. It is the most commonly mis-cited licence in the field — “MusicGen is MIT” is only half true, and it’s the wrong half for releasing music.

ACE-Step 1.5: the clean one

MIT for code and weights, with an explicit commercial-output grant on the model card. Watch community fine-tunes instead: ACE-Step Studio notes a “Regrind” fine-tune under CC BY-NC-SA — derivatives don’t inherit the base model’s licence.

Stability family: the $1M line

The Community Licence allows free commercial use only below $1M annual revenue; at or above that you need Enterprise. Stable Audio Open 1.0’s launch terms were stricter (non-commercial research), so version-pin what you download. Stable Audio 3 Medium is additionally gated by Gemma terms for its text encoder.

DiffRhythm’s VAE footnote

The repo licence is Apache 2.0 for “code and DiT weights” — but the bundled VAE derives from Stable Audio Open. A redistribution or commercial analysis should check that component’s terms separately.

Muse: permissive licence, borrowed data

MIT/Apache/MIT on paper — but the training corpus is 116k synthetic Suno-v5 outputs. Whether derivative use is permissible under Suno’s terms of service is not addressed in the paper.

TangoFlux & Mustango: assume nothing

Neither is a clean permissive licence on the evidence found — Stability-community and dataset restrictions for TangoFlux, and Mustango’s licence unverified from its primary repo. Do not assume Apache for either.

The closed-source bar, briefly: Suno v6 (launched 9 Sep 2026; Pro $10/mo, $8 annual, 20 downloads/mo cap; Premier $30/$24, 60 downloads) remains the quality reference for finished vocal songs — Suno v5.5 was #1 on the Artificial Analysis vocals arena before v6 replaced the lineup, and no open model has an equivalent independent arena win. ElevenLabs Music v2.5 (11 Sep 2026, $0.15/min, API available) is the licensing-clean/API reference. Udio’s downloads have been disabled since its Oct 2025 UMG deal, making it currently unusable for deliverable files. None of the three is locally runnable.

08

What couldn’t be verified

Stated plainly, because a report that hides its gaps is worse than one that lists them.

  • Stable Audio Open 1.5’s primary specs — no Stability announcement or HF card was retrieved; every 1.5 figure here is secondary, and its differentiation from 1.0 is unverified.
  • YuE v1’s licence on current HF cards — repo and secondary sources say Apache 2.0 since 30 Jan 2025, but some model-card mirrors still display CC BY-NC 4.0.
  • DiffRhythm 2’s VRAM, sample rate and 2026 maintenance — none stated in its README; no post-Oct-2025 activity was visible.
  • InspireMusic’s weights licence, and whether InspireSong was ever released — the README lists the model with no link and states only music generation is supported.
  • Mustango’s licence and exact max duration/VRAM — not verified from its primary repo this run.
  • MuLaCover’s licence, VRAM and speed — announced in the HeartMuLa repo (16 Sep 2026) with a separate repo that was not opened.
  • YuE2’s max length — the upstream semantic budget implies ~6 min; YuE2 Studio claims up to 10 min and ComfyUI exposes 900 s with an explicit “untested” note in secondary coverage. No primary source confirms quality beyond 6 min.
  • YuE2 languages beyond English/Mandarin — studio samples include es/ja/ru, which is community evidence, not an upstream guarantee. Likewise ACE-Step’s “50+ languages” is an upstream claim with no per-language quality breakdown.
  • HeartMuLa’s single-GPU VRAM — upstream gives only 2-GPU / lazy-load guidance; the ~12 GB fp16 / ~6 GB 4-bit figures come from a community ComfyUI project.
  • CPU generation times on the Dell’s exact hardware — no source benchmarks an Intel i5 + integrated graphics for any vocal model. Every timing in §04 beyond YuE2 Studio’s “many times slower” is a labelled estimate.
  • HF Space guest quotas — no durable published quota was found for any demo; one YuE2 community Space was reported unavailable in Sep 2026.
  • LeVo 2’s post-April-2026 status — the repo fetch available to this research was crawled 14 Apr 2026; the Jun 2026 “LeVo 2” arXiv submission is known only via search index. Recheck the repo and LICENSE before quoting post-April status.
  • RunPod Secure 4090 pricing — trackers disagree ($0.69–0.74 vs $0.89) in Oct 2026; Community $0.34 and Vast ~$0.41–0.49 are concordant.
  • Khala and Muse — covered from one independent secondary source (IT-JIM) plus Muse’s arXiv abstract only; their repos were not opened. Treat their rows as provisional.
